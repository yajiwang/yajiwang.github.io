Center for Collective Learning

Corvinus University of Budapest

I am a computational social scientist working at the intersection of large-scale social media data and large language models.

I combine behavioral data from X (Twitter) and Bluesky with bibliometric, citation, and altmetric databases to study how knowledge is produced, how it spreads, and who gets left out.

About

My current work is centred on the empirical evaluation of LLMs in social research: comparing LLM and human reference selection, measuring gender bias in LLM-driven knowledge production, and mapping the limits of LLMs for name-based inference.

I am part of the Computational Inequalities Research Group, funded by the European Union under the Horizon EU project LearnData (101086712) and led by Orsolya Vásárhelyi. Before Budapest I completed my PhD in Science of Science and Management of Science and Technology at Dalian University of Technology, held a RESPECT research fellowship at the Georgia Institute of Technology, and spent two years as a visiting PhD researcher at the University of Copenhagen supported by the China Scholarship Council.

LLMs in knowledge production

How do language models choose what to cite? I compare LLM-selected and human-selected references at scale, and measure the prestige, gender, and recognition biases that survive verification.

Scientific careers & mobility

How research trajectories shift around a first disruptive paper, and how national mobility shapes career performance and global academic inequality.

Altmetrics & scholarly communication

Diffusion of scholarly books and articles on social platforms: publishers as publicity agents, authors as self-promoters, and the networks that carry academic content.
